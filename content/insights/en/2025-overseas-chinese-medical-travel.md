The tide of "returning to China for medical treatment" is rising among overseas Chinese communities, and the data reveals the core logic behind this trend — unbeatable efficiency and cost advantages that outperform the UK and Australian healthcare systems.

Let's look at the striking contrasts that matter most to patients:

## Waiting Time Gap

Non-urgent surgeries in Australian public hospitals take 3-12 months on average (Better Health, Victoria Government). In the UK, routine specialist appointments can take up to 6 weeks. However, in China's top tertiary hospitals, most surgeries can be arranged within a week, with one-stop services covering examinations, operations, and post-op observation. No more waiting anxiety.

## Cost Advantage That's Hard to Ignore

A dental implant costs 6,000-8,000 AUD (≈28,000-37,000 RMB) in Australia, but only 15,000-25,000 RMB in China's first-tier cities. Cataract surgery in the UK costs around £3,000, while in China it's only 1/3 of that. For benign tumor resection, the total cost in Australia's private hospitals is 3,000-5,000 AUD (≈14,000-23,000 RMB), but less than 5,000 RMB in China's tertiary hospitals.

## Policy Support Smoothing the Journey

Beyond the numbers, policy support makes the journey smoother: China has optimized cross-border medical processes, canceling GP referral requirements, launching cross-border medical insurance settlement pilots in Guangdong, Zhejiang, Shanghai, and other regions, and providing exclusive services like English medical record translation and overseas medical record docking.
